# Geometric logo construction

Read this when a symbol needs coherent geometry or a visible construction rationale. Brand-specific measurements belong in that project's brief and recipe, not in these shared principles.

## Reference evidence

- [Google Design: Evolving the Google Identity](https://design.google/library/evolving-google-identity) describes geometric letterforms and optical refinement of the compact G. Use a geometric scaffold, then judge small-size perception. It is a documented 2015 design case, not a claim about every current Google asset.
- [Pentagram: MIT Media Lab](https://www.pentagram.com/work/mit-media-lab) describes a shared 7×7 grid for the monogram and research-group glyphs. A common grammar helps an identity extend beyond one drawing.
- [IBM: 8-bar](https://www.ibm.com/design/language/ibm-logos/8-bar/) supports optical weight adjustment and consistent clearspace across uses.

## Decisions that matter

Choose a few primitives that explain the actual silhouette: rectangles, circles or circular arcs, and a small set of angles. Define a base unit and relate stem weight, counter size, cap height, radius, and spacing to it. Keep the counter as deliberate as the positive shape.

A grid is useful when it controls the paths. Do not place decorative circles over a finished arbitrary drawing and call that its construction. Avoid presenting a golden-ratio overlay as proof of quality. Not all successful logos are geometric, and optical adjustments may legitimately depart from the scaffold.

Prefer filled vector silhouettes or deliberate boolean unions for production. Multiple overlapping strokes, mismatched caps, and a separately attached arrowhead often create lumpy joins. Check the single-color silhouette before introducing color. A detail that reads only in a large construction diagram is not reliable in a header.

Keep a construction diagram with actual centers, radii, and axes. Separately show the production asset without guides at realistic sizes. Document any optical departure and the observed problem it corrects; do not invent a mathematical justification afterward.

For revisions, distinguish an established brand invariant (name, recognizability, agreed palette) from revisable geometry. Explicit user direction to refine a symbol takes precedence over preserving every old path.
